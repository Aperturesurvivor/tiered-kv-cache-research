All timed sparse routes matched their respective validation events. The median of paired device/CPU serving ratios was 0.797 at 8K and 0.860 at 32K; device/stock ratios were 12.889 and 5.237. The device scoring path improved this CPU implementation in these measurements, but still took substantially longer than stock even at 32K. Its forced-token step times were also slower than stock. Accounted service is a sum of mount, initialization, serving, checkpoint and cleanup timers, not a continuous wall measurement. It excludes shared prefix construction and post-timer serialization. Prefix construction wall was 5.649 s at 8K and 33.849 s at 32K, once per length; index build is already inside initialization.

At 32K the median post-reset CUDA allocated peak was 4,212,381,184 bytes for stock and 3,310,586,368 for device, covering serving through checkpoint; initialization peaks are reported separately. Device's configured extra candidate-cache bound was 117,440,512 bytes and its CPU bound metadata was 14,737,408 bytes. Median logical store reads were 880,431,104 bytes for stock and 5,338,193,920 for device; these are not physical SSD traffic. Sampled host RSS and all individual repetitions, steps, I/O and telemetry appear in [derived-costs.json](derived-costs.json).

Within each length all arms received identical prompt and forced IDs (166 model-token evaluations: 102 prompt and 64 forced). The 8K and 32K fixtures can differ in prefix, prompt and forced IDs, so across-length differences do not isolate length or establish asymptotic scaling. These are warm-cache measurements with three correlated repetitions and a single fixture per length. Stock batches the prompt while sparse processing is token-by-token; sparse arms also incur route/position-hash observation. Those differences are part of this implementation comparison and do not remove the observed slowdown. It is not an answer-quality, statistical-significance, physical-SSD, or one-million-token result.
